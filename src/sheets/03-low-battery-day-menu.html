The Low-Battery Day Menu

Bad days are not planning emergencies; they're restaurants without a reservation. What you want is a menu — already written, decisions already made, nothing to figure out while you're running on fumes.

This sheet is that menu. It's organized by how much battery you actually have, not by how much a planner says you should have. On a 20% day, a 20% item is a complete success. Anything above it is a bonus, never an expectation.

  • 1Fill in each battery band on a good day, while you're kind. Do not write menus on bad days; write them on kind ones.
  • 2Include the boring wins: shower, eat something, meds taken. Those count. They've always counted.
  • 3On the bad day itself: find your band, pick one item, check it off. One is a day. You may not add a second one to the first one, but you're allowed to if it happens.

A finished low-battery day looks like a small, barely-scribbled page. Put it on the fridge anyway. That's what honest success looks like mid-burnout.

This sheet is self-care logistics, not medical advice. Use it alongside whatever your own care team teaches you.

0–25% — Survival floor

If you do nothing else today, the floor holds. Nothing below this is negotiable-in-a-good-way; all of it is enough.

  • Eat one thing with actual food in it
  • Drink water: one glass, refilled once
  • Meds / any daily medical routine — done or set where future-me will trip over it
  • Bed stays made-ish, or stays shamelessly stayed in
  • Pick one comfort thing (show, blanket, song, pet) — scheduled, unapologetically
  • Tell one person the honest battery level (optional but useful)

Other floor items for me:

25–50% — Battery saver

  • Ten minutes of surroundings better: one surface cleared, trash out, dishes soaked
  • Reply to the one message that's blocking someone else
  • Body gets one gentle thing: walk to the corner, stretch on the floor, shower-with-the-door-closed
  • One "level 1" task from the matcher sheet
  • Screens get one deliberate boundary: an alarm, a timer, a room change

Other saver items for me:

50–75% — Slow and steady

  • One real chore from the pile (name it while battery exists: )
  • Two tasks at level 2 or one at level 3 from the matcher
  • The thing I've been postponing anyway gets five honest minutes — just five
  • Meal with more than one color in it
  • Tomorrow-me care: one note, one laid-out item, one calendar glance

Other steady items for me:

75–100% — Regular power

  • One level 4 or level 5 task — a bonus, never a debt
  • Spend a little surplus on future-me: pre-chop, pre-write, pre-wash
  • Spend a little surplus on someone: a kind message, a favor, a call
  • Something I do for no reason except that it's good
