The Realistic Hours-Per-Day Tracker

This is a coloring page at heart, but with one rule, and the rule is the whole point: only color minutes that actually happened.

Not planned minutes. Not "well, I was ~sort of working" minutes. Real, actual, happened minutes. Honest coloring is the exercise. If your colored blocks look smaller than your good intentions did on Sunday night — good. That's the tracker working. Most people see a gap between planned and actual of about 2–3 hours a day, which means your plans were never the problem; they were fiction with deadlines.

Once it's colored honestly for a week, you'll know the one number every planning system quietly needs and almost nobody measures: your real hours. You'll plan from that number from now on.

  • 1Track in one-hour blocks, marked off the page like rings on a tree.
  • 2Each evening, color one block per hour you actually spent on the day's named commitment. Partial hours: color a partial block, lightly. Rounding up is planning practice; coloring is reporting practice.
  • 3At the week's end, read the honest columns. Not the biggest column — the most common one. That's your real weekly capacity, and you carry it forward.

Fair warning: your first honest week may feel like a damage report. By week two or three it starts feeling like a superpower. Real numbers can't be disappointed by you.

Copy it weekly. Leave the whole sheet around where you eat breakfast, and color forgetful days before bed.
